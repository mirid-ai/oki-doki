# Wrench & Bandage Field Repair

This optional in-game offer is a playful engineer-and-medic-themed field-repair kit. The offer includes a name-based free claim without identity verification. Matching ignores letter case and surrounding or repeated whitespace.

In the current episode, the offer appears at the arcade and can be skipped. An eligible name lets a player claim the kit for free; its wrench and bandage have a short story payoff after the ending choice. The entered name is not saved or transmitted by the offer. If the player chooses to save their game, the free-kit flag is stored with that local save; this is not a store entitlement and does not sync across devices.

For anyone else the intended price is the **lowest paid price the applicable app store permits**, selected for the product's storefront and shown using the store's current localized price. Store pricing differs by currency and region, and can change. Apple, for example, publishes [storefront price points](https://developer.apple.com/in-app-purchase/), while Google publishes [country-specific price ranges](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10532353). Select and verify the appropriate minimum tier in each store's developer console; the price returned for an already-configured product does not by itself establish that it is the minimum.

`src/repair-kit.js` evaluates the free-name offer. The game can also call an experimental native store bridge to show a store-provided price, purchase or restore ownership; only a verified purchased response from that bridge grants the paid kit. Pending, cancelled and failed purchases do not grant it. The bridges ship disabled and have not been tested with configured products in store sandboxes, so paid purchases are not available in the current project. Android debug builds have been made, but the store flow has not been tested on a device; the iOS app has not been built. See the Android and iOS billing setup documents under `mobile/` before attempting a store test.

If a product lookup does not respond, the web adapter stops waiting after 30 seconds and allows another lookup. It does not use that timeout to release an active purchase or restore: those flows remain single-flight until the native bridge reports a result.

The native store, rather than the game, owns the purchase record. A story checkpoint records that the kit was used in that playthrough; it is not a cross-device store entitlement. The Android bridge uses local signature checks and a Play ownership query, not a verification server. Google recommends server-side verification for stronger fraud and revocation protection. Store configuration, the selected minimum tier, purchase verification, account changes and restoration must be tested before release.
