# Oki Doki — publicity draft

## One-line pitch

**Oki Doki: The Last Ticket** is an original, open-source visual novel where a cosy arcade prize hunt becomes a psychological mystery—and a very cheerful welcome service is determined to make you agree that everything is fine.

## Pitch

**Oki Doki** began as a response to its creator's recollection of Aviva Sofia ASMR wishing for another *Doki Doki Literature Club* in her [video-game ramble](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4GXCgJ6t07g). It takes inspiration from the original's character connection, surprises and the different experiences players share, and builds a new world around them.

On a rainy night at Moonrabbit Arcade, Niko, Mira and Ash invite you into their cushion-fort headquarters for a homemade prize hunt. There is a plush moth called Mallow, a very enthusiastic welcome service, and a group of friends with their own ideas about how the evening should go. Spend time with the crew, choose what to say and what to share, and see where the night leads. The game opens with a relentlessly upbeat agreement screen; the arcade also asks which version of a welcome you prefer, then uses those choices to shape how it speaks later. As the night unfolds, its reassuring account of events becomes harder to reconcile with what the group can actually see.

The playable prototype has three companion routes and four main outcomes. Its written story works without an AI service. Players who start the included desktop model or connect a compatible service can also write their own messages in selected conversations and get generated character replies; the main story and outcomes are authored. AI responses are experimental and may be inconsistent. The game includes distinct Kokoro voices, with prerecorded fixed lines and local speech generation for other text on supported devices. The desktop downloads also include optional local speech recognition; browser speech recognition requires a separately configured service.

This is an unofficial, unaffiliated spiritual successor with its own characters, setting and art. It is not made by or connected to Team Salvato, and Aviva Sofia ASMR is not involved in or endorsing the project.

## Short post

I started **Oki Doki** after hearing Aviva Sofia ASMR wish for another *Doki Doki Literature Club*. It's an original, open-source visual novel: a rainy arcade, a homemade prize hunt, a group of friends—and a welcome service that's a little too sure the night is going perfectly.

Choose who to spend time with and which version of a welcome you prefer. The arcade remembers how you answered. Talk in your own words in optional AI conversations, or play the written story without a model. **The Last Ticket** is a playable work in progress, and I'd love to know what you think.

Unofficial and unaffiliated with Team Salvato; Aviva Sofia ASMR is not involved and has not endorsed it.

## Before sharing

- The [desktop preview](https://github.com/mirid-ai/oki-doki/releases/tag/v0.1.0-preview) and [source repository](https://github.com/mirid-ai/oki-doki) are public. The site is deployed on GitHub Pages and the creator's domain **odlc.io** resolves to it over HTTP. At the last check its HTTPS certificate was still pending; confirm secure access and browser gameplay before using the domain as a public play link.
- The creator has supplied **surelyitstimeyou@pm.me** for feedback; it also appears at the end of the game. The address can be included in a post if desired.
- The prototype has been played in desktop browsers. Android packages have been built and earlier builds exercised on an Android emulator, but gameplay on a physical Android device and an iOS build remain unverified; do not advertise phone compatibility as established.
- The package contains prerecorded Kokoro clips and a local speech model, tested in desktop Chrome and Linux Electron. Native Windows and phone playback are not yet verified. A publisher-supplied Chatterbox reference and its assigned speaker have not yet been configured.
- The optional field-repair offer can be claimed free with certain names. Paid store purchases are not enabled or tested; do not advertise a working paid microtransaction.
